The situation in one line

Cordilla has a working prediction model sitting unused, and thousands of accounts nobody is calling in any particular order. This agent turns "there's a model" into a short, ranked, reasoned list an SDR or Account Manager can act on today.

What the numbers actually say

I scored the 1,200 historical accounts with the model and checked what actually happened for accounts the model rated highest vs. everyone else:

Accounts in the top 10% by predicted score converted at 26.7%, against a 6.5% average — a real, computed ~4.1x lift, not an assumed number. The honest caveats: this is measured on the same historical data the model was trained on (not a fresh held-out test), and that 6.5% baseline is itself higher than what the brief describes as real-world cold-outreach rates — so treat the relative lift as the defensible claim, not the exact 26.7% figure in production.

The trade-off worth saying out loud: the bottom 10% of accounts still convert at 1.7% — not zero. Prioritizing Hot accounts means a handful of real deals in the Cold tier get less attention. That's the actual cost of ranking, not a flaw to hide.

What happens to the 300 accounts

Every account gets scored and shown — nothing is hidden. 42 accounts (14%) have snapshot data over a year old, so the agent holds those back from automatic action (a person should double-check before calling) rather than confidently acting on stale information.

Accounts split into two tracks based on relationship: SDR_Outbound (Prospects/Suspects — cold outreach) and AM_WinBack (Former Customers). Each track gets its own tone of outreach even at the same score.

Monitoring: how we'd know if this goes quiet

The brief's own cautionary tale — a model that looked fine and quietly drifted for two quarters before anyone noticed — is the design target here. Five checks run on every batch:

CheckWhat it catchesLatest run
Input qualityMissing/stale/out-of-range data before it reaches the modelPass
Score drift (PSI)The model's output distribution silently shiftingPass (PSI ≈ 0.006)
Tier sanityThreshold or feature-pipeline breakage even if PSI looks finePass
LLM output groundedness (Inspect AI)The drafting step inventing facts not in the account's real dataPass
Business-outcome proxyPredicted vs. actual conversion, by decileNot runnable yet (no 90-day outcomes exist for this batch)
New this pass: the groundedness check uses Inspect AI (UK AISI's LLM eval framework) to verify every number a draft states actually exists in that account's real data. The mock drafter always passes by construction — this check earns its keep the moment a real generative model (Groq) is active and could invent or round a number. Verified it actually fires by injecting a fabricated draft and watching it drop to a failing score.

What would make this meaningfully better

Two structural gaps limit what's possible with just these two CSVs, both worth naming directly rather than working around quietly:

Missing today

No contact-level data

These CSVs describe accounts, not people — no name, email, or phone. An agent can rank a company all day, but a rep still can't act without a contact. Any real deployment needs this joined in from Salesforce contacts/leads before "call this account" becomes literally actionable.

Unlocked by contact data

LLM-powered deep research per contact

With a real name and company, an LLM-with-tools step could pull recent news, funding, leadership changes, or LinkedIn activity before a call — turning "high intent score" into an actual conversation hook. This is exactly the kind of LLM tool-use step this agent's architecture already isolates safely (see the mock/Groq drafter design).

Missing today

No invoice / product / line-item data

Real RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary value) segmentation — especially valuable for Former Customer win-back — needs purchase dates and amounts. Nothing here supports it; see DOMAIN-DICTIONARY.md section 5 for exactly what's missing and why the current columns can't substitute for it.

Unlocked by transaction data

Deal-size-aware prioritization

Right now every Hot account is treated as equally valuable. With line-item/ACV data, tiering could weight by expected deal size, not just conversion probability — a account with 15% conversion odds and a $50k ACV may outrank one with 20% odds and a $5k ACV.

Other extensions worth considering

  • CRM write-back — push the worklist into Salesforce as Tasks/a list view reps already use, instead of a second dashboard to check.
  • Capacity-aware cutoffs — size the Hot tier to what reps can actually work this week, not just a fixed 10%.
  • Suppression rules — respect recent-contact and opt-out policy once that data is available, so the agent doesn't recommend re-contacting someone who just said no.
  • Outcome feedback loop — once the business-outcome-proxy check has real data, use it to recalibrate tier thresholds over time, not just alert.
  • SLA on Hot accounts — track how fast Hot-flagged accounts actually get worked; a Hot account sitting untouched for two weeks is going stale in exactly the way this agent flags for input data.
